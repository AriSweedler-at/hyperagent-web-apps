Synthesized 2026-09-25 from the four sweeps in this folder: `platform-apis.md` (612 lines), `how-games-do-it.md` (460), `board-games-web.md` (159), `our-page.md` (183), plus `shots/` and `shots/measurements.json`. The repo was measured at 25cb36c (main), research only. Every URL below was fetched by a sweep on 2026-09-25; the date in parentheses is the one the page states. UNVERIFIED marks a claim no sweep confirmed from a fetched page.

## 1. The ask and the answer in ten lines

Ari, 2026-09-25, verbatim: "I want backgammon to work better on a phone. It's in a browser, but it should be sideways. I want to be able to have it fit on 1 screen so you don't have to scroll around to make it work. The 'pass the phone' thing should naturally follow as the phone will be held sideways and it should lock the user into place to make it sideways. Only on mobile! how do other people do this? Do research on this if necessary."

1. A web page cannot lock an iPhone sideways. Safari on iOS has no `screen.orientation.lock()` (WebKit `ScreenOrientationLockingAPIEnabled: defaultValue: false` on main as of 2026-09-25; caniuse Aug 2026: Safari iOS 3.2-27.2 not supported), no element fullscreen (WebKit bug 206854 still NEW, 2026-06-08), and ignores manifest `orientation` (BCD: Safari iOS `false`).
2. Android can be locked, in two ways: in one tap handler `await document.documentElement.requestFullscreen(); await screen.orientation.lock('landscape')`; or an installed PWA (a WebAPK) with `"display": "fullscreen", "orientation": "landscape"`, which Chrome honours from the manifest with no JS (BCD `manifests.webapp.orientation` Chrome Android 39, fetched 2026-09-25); a TWA additionally bakes it into AndroidManifest.xml (https://pwatoapp.com/blog/pwa-screen-orientation-display-modes-android, 2026-08-16). The tap-handler lock ends with fullscreen: a back swipe runs the spec's fully-unlock steps (Chromium `DidToggleFullscreenModeForTab`), and on that exit `fullscreenchange` `document.fullscreenElement` is already null, so `lock()` rejects (Chromium `FULLSCREEN_REQUIRED`; WebKit "Locking the screen orientation is only allowed when in fullscreen", `ScreenOrientation.cpp` main, fetched 2026-09-25) and `requestFullscreen()` needs transient activation (https://fullscreen.spec.whatwg.org/, 2026-07-17; MDN 2026-09-11), so no listener can re-enter. The lock is gone until the next tap, and one exists every turn: the curtain's Roll tap (`#curtainBtn`, `web/shared/ui/curtain.ts:42`) and the roll modal's CTA (`#rollModalBtn`, `page.ts:418`).
3. So on the web "lock" means: lock where the platform allows (Android), and everywhere else a portrait gate, a full-screen "turn your phone sideways" card under `@media (orientation: portrait) and (pointer: coarse)` with the board `inert` behind it. Of the 2026 precedents, gelita (2026-09-24) does exactly this; backgammon-baddie (2026-09-24) gates by width (`(orientation: portrait) and (max-width: 640px)`) and adds `inert`; pokesol (2026-09-17) hides the canvas; oskol (2026-09-15) regrids only, no gate. No open-web board game surveyed locks.
4. Today Sheshbesh at 844x390 paints its portrait (stood-on-end) board at the 44px floor: 636px tall in a 390px viewport, 416px below the fold, both home boards and the controls off screen, the roll modal's "Buen mazal! roll" button at y=453. The curtain already fits (it is a viewport overlay: 420x246 at y=72).
5. Fitting on one screen is a third grid template: the desktop's flat two-row areas at a phone-landscape geometry, chrome moved into two side columns (~45px each once a 390x844 iPhone's 47px landscape insets are budgeted, §6.6; or top and bottom with 134px points), 44px points, `--point-len` 175 with the phone's compressing `--stack-step`. All additive CSS under `(max-width: 899px) and (orientation: landscape)`; the `:1795` scroll fallback narrows to portrait.
6. The curtain's markup needs no change; its Roll tap is also the gesture C re-locks from after a back swipe (§5C). The roll modal is a `#board` child (`.roll-modal { position: absolute; inset: 0 }`), so it fits once the board does.
7. "Only on mobile" is `(pointer: coarse)` (+ `(hover: none)`) and a height ceiling (`max-height` 540-600px, the lichess/baddie breakpoint), never UA sniffing and never `'lock' in screen.orientation` (present on desktop Chrome where it always rejects, absent on iPhone).
8. Recommendation: A (third template) + D (dismissible portrait gate on the table screen only) + C-lite (best-effort Android fullscreen+lock behind the Start tap and re-run from every curtain Roll tap, next to the existing wake-lock effect). Skip B (CSS-rotate the page): every Western engine community rejects it, ACT rule b33eff fails it, and it fights our body-level overlays.
9. Desktop at 1280x800 and the 390x844 goldens stay byte-identical if every new rule sits under the landscape query and the gate is scoped to `(pointer: coarse)`, which headless Chromium without `hasTouch` reports false (measured 2026-09-25, §5D). A landscape desktop window under 900px wide is a behaviour change (§6.5).
10. Effort: one measurement step on Ari's phone (§7 PR 0), then three PRs, the first (CSS template + `layout.ts` third layout + e2e `phone-landscape` viewport + docs) is the bulk; the gate and the Android lock are each small.

Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 F100 (2026-01-12) names a "rotate your device" message a failure of SC 1.3.4 unless the orientation is essential (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F100.html); ACT rule b33eff (2025-07-08) fails `@media (orientation: portrait) { html { rotate: 90deg; } }` outright (https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/b33eff/). Keeping today's portrait board reachable behind a dismiss keeps us compliant.

Recurring patterns: (1) nobody on the open web locks; they re-grid on `(orientation: landscape)` or gate portrait; (2) in landscape the board is sized from the height and the chrome becomes a side column; (3) the phone-landscape breakpoint is keyed on height (under 540-600px) so tablets are not mistaken for phones; (4) the 2026 projects scope on `(pointer: coarse)`, not width alone; (5) no surveyed board game combines landscape with a pass-the-phone handoff (a survey gap, not a finding); the same-device two-player genre (Smily Volley, Pong Online) holds the phone in landscape between two players and puts each player's controls at their own short edge, the only precedent for §6.7 chirality and for chrome-per-player placement; (6) viewport units beat CSS `zoom` (BGA's Chrome 128 breakage).

### C. A real lock: Android fullscreen + `lock()`, optional manifest

- Portrait phone: on Android, after the Start/Join tap the tab is fullscreen and locked landscape, so portrait never appears, and this holds with the player's auto-rotate off (`SENSOR_LANDSCAPE`, §2); on iPhone, no effect (D covers it).
- Landscape phone: A, with the system bars gone (insets drop to 0 in fullscreen).
- Lock: in the Start/Join handler (already a user gesture) `try { await document.documentElement.requestFullscreen(); await screen.orientation.lock('landscape'); } catch {}`; unlock and exit on leaving the table. No listener can re-lock: on the exit `fullscreenchange` `document.fullscreenElement` is already null, so `lock()` rejects, and `requestFullscreen()` needs transient activation (§1.2), so after a back swipe the lock is gone until the next tap. The taps that exist every turn are the curtain's Roll (`#curtainBtn`) and the roll modal's CTA (`#rollModalBtn`): the effect runs the same try block from both, which is the curtain behaviour C needs. Fullscreen `document.documentElement`, not `#app`: `#curtainOverlay`, `#resultOverlay`, `#menuOverlay`, `#toast` and the `.flyer`/`.drag-ghost` clones are `<body>` siblings of `#app` (our-page.md §4.1 B), so a fullscreen `#app` would hide the curtain itself. Manifest `"display": "fullscreen", "orientation": "landscape"` adds OS-level landscape for an installed Android app; iOS ignores both members.
- Curtain: its Roll tap doubles as the re-entry gesture after an exit (above); fullscreen also removes the address bar, which is the one-screen win on Android.
- Desktop untouched: gate on `(pointer: coarse)` or `navigator.maxTouchPoints > 0`; desktop Chrome's `lock()` always rejects and the catch absorbs it.
- Code: a shell effect beside the wake lock: `web/shared/ui/shell.ts:386,427,810,1382,1389`, `web/shared/ui/shellEffects.ts:37`, `web/shared/edge/boot.ts:393,456-458` (a `createOrientationLock(docLike, screenLike)`); a manifest needs `web/games/backgammon/page.ts` `blocks.head` (:71-98) + `node --experimental-strip-types tools/shell-markup.ts --write`, a `manifest.webmanifest` emitted `./`-relative, and icons.
- Tests: `web/shared/ui/shell.test.ts`, `web/games/backgammon/src/ui/state.test.ts`; for the manifest `test/dist/shell-markup.test.ts`, `asset-urls.test.ts`, `check-dist-paths.test.ts` and smoke ("fails on any failed request"); Vite's `renderBuiltUrl` handling of `link[rel=manifest]` is UNVERIFIED. One e2e can cover the wiring: `page.addInitScript` ("evaluated after the document was created but before any of its scripts were run", https://playwright.dev/docs/api/class-page#page-add-init-script) stubs `document.documentElement.requestFullscreen` and `screen.orientation.lock` to record calls, then asserts both are called inside the Start tap and the curtain tap and released on leave; only the real fullscreen and rotation stay manual.
- Risks: a back gesture or swipe exits fullscreen and silently unlocks (spec: exiting fullscreen runs the fully-unlock steps) and nothing re-locks before the next tap; Android shows a "swipe down to exit" hint on entry; `display: fullscreen` hides all system UI in the installed app; Android tablets may report lock unsupported; nothing can lock before the first tap.
