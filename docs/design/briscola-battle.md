# Briscola battle: authentic cards, tap-to-draw, the clash, the trick chip, the live intent mirror

Design only, 2026-09-25. Nothing here is implemented; every PR in §7 lands AFTER the page's first deploy. Synthesised from four read-only sweeps (`authenticity.md`, `research.md`, `choreography.md`, `live-intent.md` in this folder) measured on main `0ec147d` and the `briscola-page` branch (`scratchpad/bg/wt-j`, `66880e2`). ## 1. The ask and the answer

### 1.1 The owner's words (2026-09-25, verbatim)

1. "it is important to me that the aspect ratio of the briscola cards feels authentic. As well as the rules (draw order, etc.)"
2. "and as part of fit & finish, let's add some nice stuff. You have to tap to draw the card. There is an animation of the card going to your hand and flipping over. When you play your card it is in the middle visible to all, animated from your hand to the middle. And when the 2nd person plays the same thing, but it is faster, and then the cards bump into each other like they are fighting, with an impact frame. They should feel organic. Kinda like,,, they back up a bit to charge, and then angle in and BOOM they charge and clash and there's a slashing or something impact frame (for swords, each suit can have a specialize impact frame, with additional sparkles when it is a briscola deck) the impact frame is dictated by the winner. When the cards clash, the losing one will look defeated, wilted, torn, or something. there should be many animations to keep it fresh. Take inspiration from how pokemon battles have different starting screens but they all fit the same formula. And then the two cards package up to form a trick, and line up on your side (or opponents side) in a non-obtrusive way."
3. "And ofc when you click a card but before you play it, the opponent will be able to see it as highlighted (or pushed up) when you hover your cards the opponent will also be able to see it (as highlighted)"
4. "There are no undos when you select a discarded card and then decide against it - unlike in gin rummy which allows for slower more thoughtful play"
5. "all of that briscola stuff should happen AFTER the first deploy"

Standing theme rule: subtle but recognizable; the cards may be as ornate as the pack.

### 1.2 The answer in ten lines

1. Aspect: the napoletane box (0.577 w/h, the Commons redraw's cells after a 6 px inset) is 4 to 7% too narrow against the printed card (Dal Negro 51×82, Modiano 51×83.5, it.wiki 50×83: 0.60 to 0.62). Fix: a per-pack physical box (`card: {w: 51, h: 83}` → 0.614) with the picture `background-size: contain` centred in it, never stretched and never via the `inset` knob (which distorts); widen `ASPECT_RANGE` to [0.47, 0.70]. linea (0.518) and american (0.694) are honoured in kind.
2. Rules: draw order (winner first, trump card to the last drawer), lead (right of the dealer), direction (counter-clockwise), trump placement (half under the stock), scoring and 61/60-60 all match the Italian sources. The only real deviations sit inside optional flags (exchange window, partner peek). Cosmetic wins: one-card-at-a-time deal animation, a cut-for-deal flourish, one line of signals copy.
3. Tap-to-draw is a picture gate, not an engine step: `resolveTrick` already draws inside the n-th play, so the card is in `view.me.hand` when the resolving frame paints. Only MY draw waits for my tap; every other seat auto-draws in `drew` order around it. No wire frame, no save change; a reload paints the whole hand.
4. The beat is one fixed formula as more stages of today's `settle` machine: DRAW(tap) → PLAY → FOLLOW → CHARGE → STRIKE → IMPACT → AFTERMATH → PACK → DRAW(auto). About 1.2 s from the completing card's paint to the chip landing, ≤ 1.65 s with the auto draws (they start 60 ms into PACK-glide at every seat count, §3.2); my tap is outside the budget.
5. The impact is hit-stop: both fighters freeze 120/160/200 ms by trick value (7 to 12 frames; the fetched fighting-game figures are 9 to 15: Sonic Hurricane 9/11/13 and SF2 14, GGST 11 to 15; SFV's 8/12/15 is a search excerpt, UNVERIFIED; the sweep's 70/90/120 is the alternative, §8), the winner's suit picks the impact frame (8 hand-drawn SVG symbols), a briscola adds sparkles, and the trick phrase's first note is the BOOM. Slashes for spade, as asked.
6. Freshness without a wire change: a 32-bit FNV-1a hash over `startedAt:gameNo:no:cardIds`, decoded in mixed radix (divmod 3·3·2·3·2·2·5·3·2, §3.3), fills the flavour slots (charge distance and angle, tempo, loser pose, winner recoil, stack side, frame A/B, sparkle shape): 6480 surfaces, uniform to 1.5 ppm, identical on every device, no `TrickData` field, no rng call. The meaning-bearing slots (suit, briscola, value) are rules, as Pokémon's transitions are.
7. Loser poses wilt, tear, fade, spin-out, flutter; the winner never wilts. PACK stacks the fan face-down onto the winner and one chip glides to a quiet strip of tiny face-down chips per side (`.seat-taken`, `#myTaken`); a tap on a strip opens the last-trick sheet.
8. Reduced motion: glides 1 ms, CHARGE/STRIKE/shake skipped, the impact a 300 ms still; `fast` is ×0.6 with floors. Everything animated is `transform`/`opacity`, one layout read per launch, `will-change` on the two fighters only.
9. The live intent mirror is one ephemeral frame `{t: 'intent', seat, slot, mode}` carrying the slot INDEX (hands are secret), 60 ms trailing throttle, host checks the seat against the channel and budgets 30/s, relays at 3 to 4 seats; the receiver lifts the slot-th back by class. It needs a ~70-line generic lane in the shared shell because the seven-frame unions and boot's `send` routing are closed.
10. Ten PRs after the first deploy, aspect first (§7). Open for Ari (§8): the freeze length, pass-and-play variant (b), 3D vs 2D flip, whether the shared-lane touch is worth it in the first post-deploy batch, hover mirrored only while the hand is live, per-device speed (D11), the exchange window (D12), quote 4's reading (Q12), phone hover (D13) and the draw stall (D14).

Reconciliations between the sweeps: the wire frame is live-intent's `intent {seat, slot, mode}` at 60 ms (choreography's `hint {kind}` at 80 ms is superseded); the freeze follows research P5/P7 (longer) with choreography's numbers kept as the alternative; the shake is the winner's 1 to 2 px vibrate (research P6) rather than a `#trick` shake, so the arena never moves (P17, theme rule); the stage looks are CSS classes driven by the reducer's clock (choreography), with WAAPI held in reserve for anything that must be cut mid-flight (research P18/P19).

## 3. The beat

Two facts from the branch shape it. (a) `engine/apply.ts resolveTrick` draws INSIDE the n-th play (winner first, `seatsFrom(n, winner)`, the trump card to the last drawer when `stock.length === n`), so there is no draw action and the drawn card is already in `view.me.hand` when the resolving frame paints. (b) That frame is the first moment any device knows the outcome, so the completing card's flight, the clash and the phrase all start from one paint. Today the beat is `Settle {stage: 'hold' | 'fly' | 'draw'}` with one `settle` timer and `HOLD_MS 900 / FLY_MS 320 / DRAW_MS 260 / DRAW_GAP_MS 160` (state.ts:281-284); the battle beat is more stages of that machine, not a second one.

### 3.1 The formula (fixed) and the phases

```
DRAW(tap) -> PLAY -> FOLLOW -> CHARGE -> STRIKE -> IMPACT -> AFTERMATH -> PACK -> DRAW(auto for the others)
|   before the trick   |            the clash, from one paint           |     the settle       |
```

- DRAW (mine, tap-gated): tap `#stock` (or `#briscola.tappable` when I take the trump card), my `.slot.empty.awaiting`, or anywhere on `#hand`. A back flies stock → slot (240 ms), lands, and the slot's `.flip` turns `rotateY(180deg)` (220 ms) to the face. The seats before me in `drew` draw as the stage opens; the seats after me draw after my card lands, so winner-first order holds on every device. No timeout; a NEWER view (the winner led while I dawdled) collapses the wait at `fast` speed in the same paint as the new play's flight.
- PLAY: the first card leaves a hand (mine from its slot, measured before the repaint; theirs from the seat's last tiny back) and lands in the fan at its tilt (`launch` gains `endRotate`). A drag's release lands the ghost and skips the flight.
- FOLLOW: every later card flies faster; the completing card fastest, landing 6% past its rest and skidding back over CHARGE's first 40 ms. At 3 to 4 players the non-completing followers are plain 240 ms flights with no clash.
- CHARGE: the two fighters (the winning card and the runner-up, `trickWinner` over the rest; bystanders dim to .6 and drop 4 px) translate AWAY along the axis between their centres by `charge.dist`, the winner leaning `+angle`, the loser `−0.6 × angle` (anticipation, the owner's "back up a bit").
- STRIKE: both translate TOWARD the contact point (the axis midpoint), overshooting 0.08 × `--mid-w`, ease-in, meeting on the last frame.
- IMPACT (hit-stop): both frozen; `.clash-fx` appears at opacity 1 with `steps(1, end)` at the contact point; the winner vibrates 1 to 2 px (Smash's vibrate: the attacker trembles, the arena never moves); the trick PHRASE starts here (its first note is the BOOM). "The more damage an attack inflicts, the longer the hitstop", capped (Sakurai via Source Gaming, 2015-11-11, https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/, read 2026-09-25); light/medium/heavy 8/12/15 frames in SFV (search excerpt, UNVERIFIED), Lv0 to Lv4 11 to 15 in GGST (Dustloop Frame_Data, read 2026-09-25).
- AFTERMATH: winner recoils to rest by `winner.after`; loser plays `loser.pose`; `.clash-fx` fades 200 ms; sparkles (≤ 8) rise when `briscola` and are CUT at PACK-stack's first frame, whatever the tempo. `taking` (today's lift + cream outline) goes on the winner at IMPACT and stays through PACK.
- PACK: the loser (and bystanders) slide under the winner from `stack.side` while every fan card flips to its back (140 ms); ONE chip clone glides to the winner's strip (240 ms, today's `launch` with `fit`); counters and scores tick as it lands (today's `before`).
- DRAW (auto): a back per drawer in `drew` order, today's `drawFlights`, split around my index.

Digits d0..d8 are the 32-bit hash decoded in mixed radix (arities 3·3·2·3·2·2·5·3·2 = 6480, divmod in that order), so every surface has 662,803 or 662,804 preimages (2³² mod 6480 = 3,856): bias 1.5 ppm. The earlier `bits 0-1 mod 3` / `bits 9-11 mod 5` scheme was rejected: 4 values into 3 makes dist 0.18 twice as likely as 0.24 or 0.30, and 8 into 5 makes wilt/tear/fade twice spin-out/flutter. Named rarities (research P4): briscola-on-briscola (`steal`) adds the second sparkle pulse; the last trick of a hand uses `huge` freeze regardless of points.

### 3.4 Seeding: `variantOf(view, trick, startedAt, gameNo)` = pure FNV-1a 32-bit over `${startedAt}:${gameNo}:${no}:${cardIds}` (the flavour digits, `web/shared/lib/hash.ts`, new; none on main) plus the rule bits from `facts = trickFacts(view.trumpCard.s, trick.cards)` (engine export, index.ts:52, pure over the record). The record itself lacks them: `TrickRecord`/`lastTrick` = {no, leader, cards, winner, points, drew, trumpTaken} (apply.ts:154-162), no `winningCard`, `briscola` or `steal`; the same facts sit on the last `trick` event in `view.events` (apply.ts:188-193), the fallback if a view ever drops `trumpCard`. Test: the host's and a guest's views of one trick give identical rule bits and identical digits. ## 4. The live intent mirror (online only)

### 4.1 The frame

```ts
{ t: 'intent', seat: 0 | 1 | 2 | 3, slot: 0 | 1 | 2 | null, mode: 'hover' | 'raised' }
```

`t` is every wire frame's discriminator (`WIRE_TAGS`, protocol.ts:29). Decoder `object({t: literal('intent'), seat: literal(0,1,2,3), slot: nullable(literal(0,1,2)), mode: literal('hover','raised')})`; builder `intent(seat, slot, mode)` spells the keys in wire order so the golden re-encodes byte for byte. `slot: null` clears (mode then ignored). `slot` indexes the sender's ENGINE-ORDER hand (`v.me.hand`): receivers paint `handCount` backs or `other.hand` faces from the same `State.hands[seat]`, so the index names the same card everywhere and leaks nothing (three identical backs). A card id is rejected: relayed at four players it would leak a hidden card to an opponent. ~46 bytes JSON (BinaryPack size UNVERIFIED). Ephemeral: memory in `Table` only (`hover`, `sent`, `sentAt`, `mirror[4]`, `budget[4]`), which is already "Session only: never saved, never on the wire" (state.ts:240); never the engine, `State.events`, the history panel or the replay; the seven briscola-wire goldens untouched, one NEW golden `test/fixtures/briscola-wire/intent.json`.

### 4.2 Sender: the device whose hand is live

`intentOf(app)` is derived after every reduce step: `raised` if `selected` or `drag`, else `hover` if the hovered slot is legal and `liveView(app) !== null`, else null. A wrapper around `reduce` compares before/after and emits `{type: 'send', frame}` only when it changed, the role is `host`/`guest` and a game is dealt. Throttle `INTENT_MS = 60` trailing on the shell timer `'intent'` (`Briscola.Timer` becomes `'settle' | 'intent'`), coalesced to the latest at `intent/flush`; `sent` equality stops repeats; ceiling 16.7 frames/s. `sent = null` on the rejoin paths, `host/frame` `join` while `s.game !== null` (shell.ts:956-966, where the host re-broadcasts the state) and `guest/connected` (shell.ts:1383), so a HELD raise reaches a peer that rejoined mid-hand; without it the equality check blocks the re-send while my intent is unchanged, and `tableCleared` never fires on a rejoin (only leave/lost/startLocal/handoff). Input: delegated `pointerover`/`pointerout` on `#hand` → `hover/set`, ONLY when `pointerType !== 'touch'` (a touch fires `pointerover` before `pointerdown` and `pointerout` after `pointerup`, W3C Pointer Events §3.3.3/§3.3.6, read 2026-09-25, so an ungated handler mirrors a phantom hover on every tap); `focusin`/`focusout` on a `.slot` counts as hover (keyboard parity); `@media (hover)` is not the gate (it describes the primary input, MDN, read 2026-09-25). `dom.ts pointerOf` gains a `type` field, the one edge change. Pass-and-play and the lobby send nothing.

### 4.3 Host: authoritative, last-write-wins, relaying, budgeted

The session already reports a guest frame with its channel's seat (host.ts:447): `frame.seat !== channelSeat` → dropped, not corrected. `mirror[seat]` is replaced (the channel is reliable and ordered, RTCDataChannel `ordered` default true, MDN read 2026-09-25: no sequence number). Relay as received to every OTHER connected seat via `{type: 'send', frame, seat: s}` (PR-5's `seat` on the send effect; at capacity 2 the relay is `[]` and the effect shape is untouched); the host's own hover goes out seatless to every channel. Budget `INTENT_BUDGET = 30` per `INTENT_WINDOW_MS = 1000` per seat, in the reducer over `ctx.now()` (host.ts "holds no game state"); over budget → silent drop, no toast, no close. Fan-out bounded at 90 frames/s ≈ 4.5 kB/s at four players. A gone guest: `mirror[seat] = null` and one synthetic clear relayed (the only frame the host builds rather than relays). Held-join strays are harmless and pinned.

### 4.4 Receiver: the painter lifts a back

In `paintSeats` after `ensureKeyed`: toggle `intent-hover` (4 px lift) / `intent-raised` (8 px + 2 px `var(--accent)` ring) on `.seat-cards > .card:nth-child(slot+1)`; the mirror is NOT in `seatKey` (a hover never rebuilds the cell, the rule `.card.selected` follows); nothing painted mid-beat (`b.stage !== null`); an out-of-range slot toggles nothing. The existing `.card` transition and reduced-motion rule cover both. Two CONTRACT.md rows. A rejoined peer sees the current lift within 60 ms of its `join` (host side) or its `guest/connected` (guest side) because the sender's `sent` was reset (§4.2); "the next hover fills it" is not enough, a held raise has no next hover.

## 5. Interaction rules the owner set

- Selection is free and visible: the first tap lifts (`.card.selected`, 12 px, the ring); the peer sees it as `intent-raised` on the slot's back; a hover shows as `intent-hover`. Lifting another card, `escape`, or a hand change drops it; the peer's mirror clears within 60 ms or on the view diff.
- A play is final: the second tap, `#playBtn`, `table/tap` or the drop commits `play`; the card is in the fan "in the middle visible to all"; no undo, no confirm, no take-back (owner's quote 4; A18). The mirrored lift is pre-commit and never a play. Quote 4 has no literal briscola referent ("a discarded card" is gin's); this reads it as "a played card is final", and the lift stays cancellable (`escape`, another card). If Ari meant selecting = committing, the first-tap lift, `escape` and the mirrored `raised` state all go: §8 Q12.
- The draw has nothing to undo: the card is already mine; the tap reveals it.
- Nothing about a tap, a hover or a draw wait reaches the engine, the save or the replay; the engine's `events` stay the only history.
