One phone, no internet needed. The board stays in view; a curtain says whose turn it is.

    Sheshbesh is what Sephardic Jews around the eastern Mediterranean called backgammon: six and five, the roll that gives the game its name. It was played in the cafés of Salonica, Izmir and Jerusalem on the same wooden boards their Greek and Turkish neighbours used, and it travelled with the families who left.

    This page plays two rulesets. Portes is the first game of the Greek set: no doubling cube, a gammon counts double, and the winner of the opening roll rolls again. Western backgammon adds the doubling cube, the triple for a backgammon and the Crawford rule in a match. Pass one phone between you, or open a table and share the code.
